03 — Special Exams

Four problems I chose to sit for.

Each one had a rule I couldn't negotiate with — a memory budget, a deadline, a dataset that lied. The result column is what survived.

01

Kernel Efficiency Exam

Took a naive matrix-multiply and rewrote it four times — tiling, shared memory, then a Triton version — profiling each against the roofline instead of guessing. The write-up is the part I'm proud of.

CUDATritonNsightC++
3.4×over the naive kernel, at 71% of theoretical bandwidth
02

Price–Performance Exam

Scraped fourteen months of retail GPU listings across three regions, normalised the mess, and modelled cost per TFLOP over time. It answers one question well: when is a card actually cheap?

PythonPlaywrightDuckDBPlotly
2,140SKUs tracked; 14 months of daily prices
03

Earnings Language Exam

Sixty semiconductor earnings calls from Intel, AMD and NVIDIA, scored for hedging language, then tested against the guidance that followed. Hedging predicts a miss better than sentiment does.

PyTorchspaCyscikit-learn
0.71AUC on out-of-sample guidance misses
04

Attendance Forecast Exam

A small, real problem: predicting lecture attendance from timetable, weather and assessment calendars, so the department stops booking the wrong rooms. Deployed as a weekly email nobody has unsubscribed from.

LightGBMPostgresStreamlit
±4.2students mean absolute error, 12 weeks live
